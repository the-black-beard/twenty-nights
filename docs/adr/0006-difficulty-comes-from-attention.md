# Difficulty comes from attention, not from scarcity

Nights get harder by demanding the player be in more than one place, not by
making wood and stone harder to come by.

Rymdkapsel is the reference. Its pressure comes from having too few minutes and
too many places needing you, and that translates to a seven-year-old far better
than counting resources does. The concrete expressions: Big Ones walk to your
towers rather than your Home, so there are two things to defend; Flyers ignore
your walls entirely; Chewers arrive in numbers set by how much you have built.

**Consequences**: when the game is too easy, the fix is a second thing to worry
about, not a nerf. Two specific techniques earned their place and should be
reached for first — *selective invalidation* (armour a stone sword cannot touch,
rather than weakening the sword) and *bounded gnaw* (each Chewer eats exactly one
structure per Night, so losses are survivable and predictable). Avoid raising
costs or shrinking supply; that route was tried and made the game tedious rather
than tense.
